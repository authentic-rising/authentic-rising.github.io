The Authentic Kids approach

Children explore the world through play, movement, imagination and curiosity. They are constantly learning about themselves, other people and the world around them, often noticing things that we as adults might overlook.

At Authentic Kids, I work with that natural sense of wonder rather than trying to shape how children should think, feel or experience things. Through playful activities, movement and conversation, I encourage children to recoginse their emotions, explore their intution, become aware of their senses and surroundings, and feel confident expressing themselves.
This is the heart of my four areas: FEEL, NOTICE, SENSE, and TRUST

01 /

Feel

Body & emotions

Our bodies can tell us a lot about how we are feeling. Through movement and play, children learn to recognise their emotions, notice how they feel in their bodies and find ways to express what they need.

02 /

Notice

Intuition & inner knowing

Ever had a feeling about something but couldn't quite explain why? Children have those moments too. We get curious about their instincts, inner voice and experiences, helping them explore what they notice without rushing to label it.

03 /

Connect

Senses,Energy & connection

How does a place make you feel? What do you notice when you are around different people? Through sensory experiences, movement and exploration, children discover how they connect with themselves, others nature and the world around them.

04 /

Trust

Self-trust & discernment

Children need to know that their thoughts, feelings and opinions matter. We encourage them to ask questions, make choices, set boundaries and feel confident speaking up for themselves.

Sensitivity, Intuition & Inner Knowing

Some children seem to feel everything

They pick up on other peoples moods, notice the smallest of changes or become overwhelmed by things others barely notice.
They may get upset easily, have a vivid imagination, or and ask questions that sometimes catches us adults completely off guard. Others talk about strong gut feelings, vivid dreams or experiences they can't quite explain.
I, too, have had experiences like this myself since childhood, and they've always made me woonder just how much we notice without even realising it.
Sensitvity can show up in so many different ways, and while it can be a wonderful part of who a child is, it can also leave them feeling overwhelmed, misunderstood or unsure of themselves.


That's were I can help

I support children in understanding themselves, building confidence and finding their voice without feeling they have to hide parts of themselves or change who they are to fit in.

For the parent who notices Something


You know your child

You notice when something changes.
Prehaps their confidence isn't quite what it used to be. Maybe their spark doesn't seem as bright. They've become quieter, started worrying about what others think or stopped doing things they once loved.
Prehaps they are struggling with friendships, finding it difficult to say no or constantly looking for reassurance.
You might have tried to talking to them, offering encouragement or doing all the things you're told should help, but something still doesn't feel quite right, and sometimes it's hard to explain what you have noticed, especially when everyone tells you are aoing fine.
Or prehaps nothing is "wrong" at all, you simply want to nurture the connection your child already has with themselves.
You don't need to wait until things get worse to seek support, and you don't need to arrive with all the answers.
I support parents too, because understanding yourself is part of helping your child

The person behind Authentic Kids

Hi, I'm Steph.

I'm Stephanie Ashyggton, although everyone calls me Steph. I'm a Children's Wellbeing Consultant and f ounder of Authentic Kids.

My work brings together my professional background as a former Specialist Public Health Nurse, my experience as a home-educating mother of four boys, and my lifelong curiosity about intuition, sensitivity and the things we sometimes experience but cannot easily explain.

I believe children come into the world deeply connected: to their bodies, their feelings, their instincts, the people around them and, for some families, something greater.

My work isn't about telling children what to believe. It's about giving them the space to explore who they are.

With warmth and magic,
Steph ✨
